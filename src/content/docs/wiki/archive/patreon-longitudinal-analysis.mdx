This page asks a different question from the [Patreon Source
Catalogue](/wiki/archive/patreon-source-catalog/). The catalogue answers **what records
exist and where they route**. This analysis asks **how Daniel used Patreon over time,
what patterns recur across posts, and where his own writing corrects or complicates
the public-video record**.

Access labels follow the catalogue: **P** means the complete body was visible signed
out on the review date, **L** means only public metadata was visible and any body
reading comes from the private preservation copy, and **A** means the body is absent.

<Aside type="danger" title="A creator-written corpus is not independent corroboration">
  The retained bodies establish what Daniel wrote. Repetition across his posts does
  not turn a claim into a verified event, authenticate another person's words, prove
  platform action, or validate his audience and income figures. Private names,
  intimate third-party detail, medical detail, addresses, and locked media remain
  excluded even when they occur in the preservation copy.
</Aside>

## Corpus shape and edit-state caveat

The preservation set contains **136 exact post-ID matches** against the public
Patreon sitemap. It retains 135 full-body records; post `23468829` retains its title,
metadata, and a short teaser but no full body. After HTML tags and repeated whitespace
are normalized, **122 records contain non-empty author-written body text**, totalling
approximately **32,591 words**. The other full-body records are mostly image, link, or
embed entries whose body field is empty or extremely short; “full-body record” does
not mean “long essay.” Teaser text is not included in the body-word count.

Thus the 2017 “scrapbook” description is not just thematic: 32 of that year's 51
records carry Patreon's image-post type. In 2018, 44 of 46 are text posts, matching
the shift toward diary-like relationship, platform, travel, and creator-strategy
updates. Publication was also bursty: 44 of the 51 posts in 2017 arrived between
April and August, while 36 of the 46 posts in 2018 arrived from January through June.
These are publication patterns, not evidence that the underlying life events were
equally concentrated.

The metadata also records **31 posts with a `last_edited_at` value later than
`published_at`**. Most edits were close to publication, but several were much later:
the October 2, 2016 drawing-timelapse post shows an edit roughly 108 days later; six
April–May 2017 records were edited again around May 8; and the December 27, 2017
relationship/income post was edited about 25 days later. The preservation copy
contains the later retained body, not a version-by-version diff. It must not be
treated as proof of the exact wording visible on publication day.

## Creative-project archaeology

The earliest bodies document a small creator funnel rather than a simple paid image
feed. On October 2, 2016, Daniel proposed posting drawing-process videos publicly and
finished webcomic pages on Patreon. Two days later, the first _Another Boring Night
Shift_ page described a $2 route for later pages and a free, ad-supported ComicFury
mirror with fewer author comments and less interaction. In June 2017 he explicitly
reset the campaign toward artwork: public low-resolution posts, with high-resolution
files and process videos reserved for paying patrons. These are plans and offer
descriptions; the retained posts do not establish how consistently benefits were
delivered or how many people used either route.

The bodies also preserve a useful production bridge. On July 29, 2017, the caption to
[_Skiing trip_](https://www.patreon.com/Hidinginmyroom/posts/skiing-trip-13478158)
ends with two possible video ideas and says the World of Warcraft addiction story
should come first. Four days later, the public archive dates
[_How my World of Warcraft addiction ruined my
life_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gGNgt_WXa_4) to August 2. The sequence is
evidence that at least one Patreon recollection became a public-video concept; it
does not show whether the final video followed the proposed account word for word.

## Controlled disclosure—and its failure modes

Daniel repeatedly presented Patreon as a space between a fully public channel and a
private diary:

- In December 2017, he said he would previously have discussed his relationship
  doubts in a video but did not want his girlfriend to find them.
- In May 2018, he called Patreon the only remaining place where he could express
  himself fully while describing tension between his marriage and the channel.
- In June 2018, he proposed a **$3 archived-livestream reward**, yet said he would not
  include one long stream because someone might download and repost remarks he did
  not want his wife to hear.
- The October 27, 2018 public title alleged that his wife was monitoring several
  platforms and possibly Patreon. The public body supports his lodging account, not
  the monitoring allegation.
- In December 2018, Daniel alleged that a subreddit user had pledged specifically to
  redistribute Patreon-only material. No alleged redistributor response or platform
  record is in the corpus.

Patreon therefore served simultaneously as **diary, paywall, backup channel, stream
archive, and audience product**. The source itself documents Daniel's awareness that
those roles conflicted: access control could reduce an audience, but could not
guarantee secrecy once a patron received the material.

## Performance, partial invention, and per-record truth status

The bodies provide unusually direct evidence that HIMR mixed autobiography with
performance. They do **not** justify calling the entire channel fictional:

- A January 3, 2018 post about an old skit says Daniel was influenced by acted sketch
  channels and still sometimes used a little acting to make videos more interesting.
- On January 21, Daniel says a named **“Australian catfish”** story had been made up
  for views but was “half true.” The exact mixture is not resolved. That story persona
  remains separate from the [Australian Guy](/wiki/characters/australian-guy/), whom
  other Patreon posts present as an unnamed man in Daniel's account. Those later
  Patreon repetitions are all Daniel's writing and do not independently corroborate
  the narrative.
- On February 1, Daniel describes _Why I hate living in Japan_ as beginning from
  genuine complaints and becoming increasingly absurd or sarcastic during filming.
  This supports mixed framing for that video, not a finding about Japan or every rant.
- In April, while worrying that relationship stability would reduce views, Daniel
  says he could invent another argument but did not intend to fabricate one or provoke
  his wife for content.
- In February 2019, he says he made a relationship-labelled livestream private after
  viewers criticized the title as clickbait.

The reliable editorial rule is **claim by claim**: a creator admission can qualify a
specific story or production method, but it cannot retroactively mark every event as
staged—or make an admitted “half true” story factual.

## Campaign and reward-object archaeology

Retained campaign metadata places creation and publication of campaign ID `363999`
on **May 12, 2016**, months before the earliest surviving post. The captured reward
objects mix much older creation timestamps with later publication timestamps and
current labels:

| Captured reward label | Amount in retained metadata | Object created | Object published | Safe interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Level 1 Daniel | $1 | 2016-11-23 | 2022-01-02 | An old reward object present in the captured campaign state. Its captured description cannot reconstruct every earlier version. |
| Early access to my game | $3 | 2017-04-27 | 2022-01-03 | The object predates the 2022 game posts by years, so the current game label was likely applied to a reused reward object. This is an inference, not preserved tier-version history. |
| paintings | $150 | 2017-05-08 | 2022-01-02 | The label fits the 2017 art phase, but the later publication timestamp prevents a simple continuous-availability claim. |
| Free | $0 | 2023-10-12 | 2023-10-12 | A later campaign object outside the 2016–2022 post sequence. |

The tier table is a warning against reading current labels backward. Patreon object
IDs can persist while names, descriptions, prices, publication state, and benefits
change. Historical posts about a “$3 reward” are therefore not silently assigned to
the captured 2022 game tier without a dated tier snapshot.
