The HIMR Wiki uses transcripts to **find** evidence, not as evidence by default. A
published claim must say no more than its cited source can establish.

<Aside type="caution" title="A primary video is not automatic proof">
  A raw video can establish what a person said or what the recording visibly
  shows. If Daniel describes an off-camera event, the safe formulation is
  “Daniel said…” unless an independent source corroborates the event.
</Aside>

## Current archive audit

On August 25, 2026, the project preserved the then-live transcript index and metadata for
[Internet Archive item 69999](https://archive.org/details/69999) and
[item 699992](https://archive.org/details/699992). The automated reconciliation found:

| Finding                                      | Count |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----: |
| Transcript records                           | 2,227 |
| Records with an Internet Archive video URL   | 2,043 |
| Records without a video URL                  |   184 |
| Distinct linked video URLs                   | 1,919 |
| Shared video-URL groups                      |    67 |
| Records participating in those shared groups |   191 |
| Records over 250 displayed words per minute  |    55 |
| Records with a malformed displayed duration  |     4 |

These are **record counts**, not a verified count of distinct channel videos. A shared
URL may be a duplicate, a chunk of a longer stream, or a mapping error. An implausible
speech rate usually indicates suspect duration or timestamp metadata and triggers
manual review.

The table is a frozen audit of that legacy index, not a statement of present media
availability. On August 27, the project reconciled the additional Internet Archive
items [`hidinginmyroom`](https://archive.org/details/hidinginmyroom),
[`hidinginmyroom2`](https://archive.org/details/hidinginmyroom2), and
[`hidinginmyroom3`](https://archive.org/details/hidinginmyroom3). Exact normalized
title/date/platform-ID matches repaired 119 distinct dead transcript endpoints used
by the wiki. A repaired link establishes only that a candidate raw file is now
addressable; it does not validate the old transcript, authenticate the title's claim,
or mean that anyone has watched or listened to the file.

The dated source responses, hashes, normalized manifest, and audit are retained in
the maintainers' private evidence store. Future snapshots do not silently replace
earlier ones.

A separate [additional source-leads inventory](/wiki/archive/additional-source-leads/)
records later Internet Archive containers, historical account pages, fan mirrors,
commentary indexes, and unresolved URLs observed on 27 August 2026. Inclusion in that
inventory means only that a lead was supplied or its public endpoint was observed. It
does not authenticate account ownership, completeness, or copied-content accuracy.

Two newer source layers remain deliberately separate. The [Patreon Source
Catalogue](/wiki/archive/patreon-source-catalog/) distinguishes public metadata,
signed-out bodies, and access-controlled bodies retained privately; a locked
first-party body can establish what Daniel wrote, but it is paraphrased rather than
copied and cannot verify the event he described. The complete available-body review
and its corpus-wide limitations are documented separately in the [Patreon
Longitudinal Analysis](/wiki/archive/patreon-longitudinal-analysis/). The [Discord Source
Map](/wiki/archive/discord-source-map/) uses a contributor-supplied author-only export
from channels represented as public. It can establish the exported account's posted
text and message IDs, but missing replies and participants prevent it from becoming
an independent event chronology or identity proof.

## Evidence ladder

| Material                                  | How the wiki uses it                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Raw video or audio                        | Verify exact speech and visible action at a stated time                                        |
| Human-corrected segment                   | Quote or closely paraphrase only the checked passage                                           |
| Machine transcript                        | Locate a likely passage; never treat it as evidence, fact, or a quotation                      |
| Title or filename                         | Describe the archive label, not the truth of the title or date                                 |
| First-party statement                     | Attribute the statement to its author                                                          |
| Access-controlled first-party written body | Paraphrase what the author claimed while retaining its locked state; never mirror the body or assets |
| Author-only public-channel message export | Map what the exported account posted; never infer missing replies, agreement, identity, or event truth |
| Independent reliable record               | Corroborate only the facts within that record's scope                                          |
| Reviewed public community image or repost | Establish only what the complete preserved copy appears to show or say, with provenance limits |
| Other community post or wiki              | Establish community usage, record an attributed allegation, or generate a research lead        |
| AI or NotebookLM output                   | Discover topics; never cite as evidence                                                        |

Automatic captions and locally generated speech recognition are machine transcripts,
even when they were produced from the original media file. They can supply a candidate
timestamp. A spoken quotation or close paraphrase becomes **video checked** only when
a human reviewer directly listens to the raw segment; reviewing frames does not count
as listening to the soundtrack.

<Aside type="caution" title="Default machine-transcript publication rule">
  An eligible machine revision may appear publicly without a human wording
  review. Every such passage must identify the exact source and coordinates and
  display the canonical warning: **“Machine-generated and unreviewed; may be
  wrong; not a verified quotation.”** It remains an unverified research lead,
  not verified speech or proof of the underlying event. Rights, privacy,
  sensitivity, identity, and coordinate fitness are separate gates and are never
  waived by this default.
</Aside>

<Aside type="danger" title="Machine-only allegations need two warnings">
  A minimized, high-level machine-text paraphrase of Daniel's alleged statement
  may be published when the public raw source and exact ranges are identified.
  It must carry both the canonical machine warning and an adjacent **Unverified
  allegation** notice that names the attributed speaker, states the
  corroboration status, and includes a relevant response or says none was
  located. It may not be presented as a quotation. Material that fails the
  independent rights, privacy, sensitivity, identity, or coordinate gates stays
  private; this includes doxxing, leaked intimate material, sexualized claims
  involving minors, and unsupported diagnostic inference. Publication does not
  verify either the machine wording or the alleged event.
</Aside>

## Verification states

- <VerificationBadge status="unverified" /> — machine-generated, imported, or
  proposed material whose wording or underlying event has not been verified.
- <VerificationBadge status="source-matched" /> — the source identity exists and
  has been reconciled; its contents are still unchecked.
- <VerificationBadge status="video-checked" /> — the cited raw-media segment and
  surrounding context were manually reviewed.
- <VerificationBadge status="community-media-checked" /> — the complete
  available repost and post context were reviewed; no obvious substantive edit
  or credible authenticity dispute was found, but the original is not
  authenticated.
- <VerificationBadge status="corroborated" /> — an appropriate independent
  source also supports the claim.
- <VerificationBadge status="disputed" /> — credible sources conflict and the
  wiki preserves the disagreement.

## From search result to published sentence

<Steps>
  1. **Draft one atomic claim.** Avoid combining several facts under one
  citation. 2. **Locate candidate evidence.** Search the transcript archive and
  source manifest. 3. **Bind the underlying source.** Preserve the exact raw
  source, coordinates, and machine status. A human must open the segment with
  surrounding context before its wording is called checked or quoted as
  verified; unreviewed machine leads stay explicitly machine-only. 4. **If only
  a repost survives, review the whole copy.** Preserve its direct media URL,
  type, bytes, hash, post context, edit assessment, and any authenticity
  dispute. 5. **Record the evidence scope.** Mark self-report, visible action,
  metadata, independent record, corroboration, or contradiction. 6. **Resolve
  the date basis.** Keep event, recording, publication, archive, and review
  dates separate. 7. **Check sensitivity and conflicts.** Minimize private
  information and preserve materially different accounts. 8. **Apply the
  independent machine-publication gates.** Wording review is optional, but the
  exact source, coordinates, canonical warning, rights, privacy, sensitivity,
  identity, and allegation rules are not. Keep any failing material private. 9.
  **Publish with a precise citation.** Record the source ID, locator, check
  status, reviewer, and review date.
</Steps>

## Dates, contradictions, and transcript corrections

A date taken only from an archive filename is labeled **filename date**. If the exact
event or publication date cannot be established, the wiki uses a range, `circa`, or
`unknown` rather than false precision.

Video titles are not treated as conclusions. If a later source changes an earlier
account, both are dated and attributed. The wiki does not silently choose the more
dramatic version.

Quoted words are checked against the media. Unclear audio is marked `[unclear]`,
conservatively paraphrased, or omitted. Machine-transcript corrections do not alter
the preserved raw snapshot.

## Privacy and sensitive material

The wiki does not republish home addresses, private contact details, financial account
data, leaked intimate material, or non-public legal names merely because another site
contains them. An allegation may be recorded with an adjacent warning when its exact
source is attributed, its truth is labeled unverified, disputed, or corroborated, its
corroboration status is stated, and a relevant response is included or the page says
none was located. A corroborated allegation must still be labeled as an allegation and
identify the independent support. A warning does not turn the allegation into fact or
excuse doxxing, intimate material, diagnoses, threats, or harassment-oriented detail.
Machine output may support an explicitly attributed **unverified allegation** without
a wording review only under the two-warning rule above. It is never enough to mark the
speech or underlying event verified. Material that fails rights, privacy, sensitivity,
identity, or coordinate review—including sexualized claims involving a minor, leaked
intimate material, or doxxing—remains private regardless of disclaimer.

A public post or Discord message permalink is a source locator, not by itself a
licence to copy an attached image, audio, video, signed delivery URL, or
access-controlled body. Locked Patreon media remains private. A contributor has
represented the supplied Discord attachments as public and permitted their use, but
only individually reviewed, editorially necessary copies may be published; private
third-party, intimate, identifying-location, and doxxing material remains excluded.
For this review, that process admitted one metadata-stripped, Pushkin-captioned cat
image and no Discord audio or video payloads. Selected audio wording remains clearly
labelled machine transcription because no reviewer directly heard it.

The complete contributor rules are maintained in the repository's editorial policy.
