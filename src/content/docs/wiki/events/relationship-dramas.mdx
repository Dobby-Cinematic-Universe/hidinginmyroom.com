This is a chronology of **source language**, not a list of verified relationships.
The archive contains many titles using words such as “girlfriend,” “wife,” “married,”
and “divorced.” Those labels establish how an upload was presented; they do not by
themselves identify the other person, prove a legal status, or establish that two
nearby records concern the same person.

Brief dating and sexual-story titles—and the minimized Patreon map of claimed
meetings, non-meetings, and anonymous roles—are catalogued separately on [Short-Term
Encounters](/wiki/events/short-term-encounters/). That separation prevents an unnamed
one-video or one-post account from being silently attached to a recurring name on
this page.

<Aside type="caution" title="How to read this page">
  A title-level entry means only that a preserved record exists under that
  label. Spoken claims are verified only when a reviewer directly hears the raw
  audio. The high-level Daniel-attributed machine accounts below remain
  conspicuously unverified, including where two passes agree. They contain no
  machine quotation and are not evidence that a relationship event happened.
  Access-L Patreon summaries establish what Daniel wrote in the retained body,
  not another person's account or the underlying event.
</Aside>

## Source-led chronology

### 2017: repeated generic girlfriend labels

The archive includes records labelled _I got a girlfriend!_ (January 30), _I broke
up with my girlfriend_ (June 18), and _I have a new girlfriend -)_ (October 13).
Because none of those titles names the other participant, the wiki does not join them
into one biography or infer the start and end dates of an off-camera relationship.

Two independent ASR passes over each verified raw file materially agree on three
distinct creator accounts:

- In the **January** video, Daniel described an online, long-distance connection with
  someone he had not met and said he was planning an April trip to meet her.
- In the **June** video, Daniel said he had ended an unnamed relationship by message,
  citing a lack of attraction and shared interests. Near the end, he expressed guilt
  and regret over earlier promises.
- In the **October** video, Daniel repeatedly used the girlfriend label while also
  acknowledging that the two people had not agreed they were boyfriend and girlfriend.

<Aside type="caution" title="Three anonymous, unverified self-accounts">
  No reviewer directly perceived the audio, and no generated wording is quoted.
  Agreement between `base.en` and `small.en` is transcription consistency, not
  corroboration of a relationship or of any other person's words. The people
  remain unnamed, the records are not joined to one another, and a bounded
  review found no independently authenticated participant response for these
  accounts. Intimate, age-dependent, and identifying details are omitted.
</Aside>

A March 2018 record is titled _WE GOT MARRIED_. A February 2020 record is titled _I
am now Officially Divorced_. The two labels document the creator's public packaging,
not a civil marriage, a civil divorce, either event date, or the identity and account
of another party. The narrower source trail around the public name Chihiro is covered

The separate [Australian Guy source trail](/wiki/characters/australian-guy/)
reconciles another 2018 relationship-related sequence. It keeps Chihiro's
first-person account apart from Daniel's claims and records that his November
coercive-divorce and infidelity scenarios were explicitly fantasies, not events.

Two-model review supplies a more useful account without resolving the legal history.
In the March 2018 video, Daniel said that a municipal registration process had been
completed several days earlier, while acknowledging that he had no certificate to
show in the video and expected additional paperwork later. In the February 2020
video, he said that he had received an image presented as filed divorce paperwork;
he attributed the breakdown to spending more time online than with his wife and to
incompatibility, while also acknowledging uncertainty about whether the paper meant
the divorce was final or merely that papers had been received.
