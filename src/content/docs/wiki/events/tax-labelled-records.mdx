The archive contains a compact sequence of 2023 records whose titles frame a tax
problem or update. This article documents that source sequence. It does **not**
establish an investigation, a tax authority, a final liability, a penalty, or
wrongdoing. No authority decision, correspondence, court record, or other independent
primary document is cited on this page.

<Aside type="danger" title="Titles are not tax records">
  Video titles are first-party framing, and any on-camera descriptions found during
  future audio review would remain first-party claims. The wiki
  does not calculate a balance from them, decide anyone's tax residence, or label a
  mistake as evasion. Those conclusions would require evidence outside the current
  corpus and, for legal interpretation, qualified advice.
</Aside>

The first three matched media files run for more than five hours; the fourth runs for
about eleven minutes. Duration metadata does not establish whether a file was live,
edited, or focused on its title topic. The final indexed record has no reconciled raw
video, so its machine transcript remains a research lead only.

## What this chronology supports

- The archive preserves five first-person tax-problem and tax-update title labels
  between June and November 2023.
- Four of the five record identities are matched to preserved Internet Archive video.
- Any amounts, tax years, residency descriptions, proposed payment plans, or penalties
  encountered during future review must remain unpublished as wiki facts until the
  exact audio is manually checked and any changing account is represented
  chronologically.

## Evidence still needed

To promote the underlying story beyond **source matched**, an editor should:

1. listen to each cited segment in the four available raw videos;
2. preserve the exact date and whether Daniel is reading a document or recalling it;
3. attribute every amount and legal characterization to Daniel unless a primary
   record independently establishes it;
4. reconcile later updates before presenting any number as final; and
5. avoid publishing personal tax identifiers, account screenshots, or other sensitive
   financial data even if visible in a stream.
