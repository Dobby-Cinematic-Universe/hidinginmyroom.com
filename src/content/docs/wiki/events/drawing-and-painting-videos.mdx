Digital-art process videos recur in the archive: two short drawing timelapses
labelled **2016** and a longer portrait tutorial labelled **2023**. The examples
below show different stages of the artwork, rather than relying on their titles
alone. They do not establish a continuous series, artistic training, sales or the
identity of a depicted person.

## October 2016: the night-shift illustration

In *drawing timelapse on ipad pro*, the **00:00:10** frame contains a rough
figure behind a counter; **00:00:42** and **00:01:21** show a more developed
dark-haired character beside a till. At **00:01:55**, the coloured scene includes
the words **“Another boring night shift…”**. This gives the public video a visible
connection to the comic label already discussed in the
[Patreon publication history](/wiki/archive/patreon-longitudinal-analysis/),
without treating a fictional scene as proof of a real workplace incident.

## December 2016: social-event imagery

*How I feel when going to social events* presents another drawing process.
At **00:00:07**, there is a simple seated figure on white; **00:00:31** places
the figure under a hanging light. The later **00:01:00** and **00:01:25** frames
show a dark-haired figure with knees drawn up, a phone on the floor and small
conversing figures in the background. The personal interpretation belongs to
the title: the drawing is not a record of a particular gathering or evidence
of a diagnosis.

## June 2023: a portrait tutorial

The later *How to Paint a Face with Acrylic Brushes* shows a different kind of
art-process presentation. At **00:00:39**, the image is a partially painted face
against white. At **00:02:42**, it has reddish-brown hair; at **00:05:14**, a
turquoise background and additional colour are visible. The **00:07:22** frame
adds tutorial-title text to the portrait. These selected stages support a
portrait-making format, not an identification of the subject, the source of any
reference image or a judgment about the artist's skill.
