A current working hypothesis is that **“cereal” may sometimes function as a coded
reference to adult sexual activity in HIMR discussion**. That interpretation is not
yet established by directly heard audio, and the archive also contains literal food
contexts. Each occurrence therefore remains context-dependent and unresolved.

<Aside type="caution" title="Adult topic; coded language is not proof">
  This page records a working terminology hypothesis, not a settled definition. It
  does not verify any encounter, identify unnamed partners, or treat machine wording as
  speech. The platform rationale described below is an unverified rendering of
  Daniel's account and has not been heard by a human reviewer.
</Aside>

## Strongest located origin candidate

The strongest located machine-text candidate is the October 14, 2017 *5k Subscriber Celebration Live
Stream*. Its machine transcript places a substitution discussion near 00:35:15,
an announcement of future usage near 00:35:35, and later age-restriction or
monetization context near 00:37:30 and 00:43:40.

That sequence explains why researchers focus on this stream, but it is not enough to
define the term or quote any line. The raw 2:38:48 video is mapped and available; the
relevant audio is queued in the maintainers' private cereal-term review. “Strongest
located candidate” does not mean proven first-ever use elsewhere on stream or in chat.

## How the term spread through the source record

### October 16, 2017: immediate follow-up

Two days later, separate **base.en** and **small.en** passes over the public raw file
materially agree at **00:06:03–00:06:28** on a narrower attributed self-account. The
machine text renders Daniel announcing a substitute term for adult or sexual
vocabulary and connecting it to trying to avoid automatic recognition,
demonetization, and age restriction. Both passes choose the spelling serial for the
ambiguous sound.

Nobody directly heard the segment. This account is therefore unverified, is not a
quotation, and does not settle whether the sound was cereal or serial. Later creator
titles and current merchandise establish cereal as the later public spelling, but
they do not prove what was said here or when the terminology was first coined.

### 2026: the label remains in merchandise

The current [Daniel's Store](https://daniels-store-154.creator-spring.com/) shows that
“cereal” outlived the video-title sequence as public branding. On August 26, 2026,
catalogue page three displayed **nine product-variant titles** containing the word.
One checked T-shirt mockup visibly reads **“Wanna Have CEREAL?”** beneath a cereal-bowl
illustration; other listings use bowl characters and related titles.

That is stronger evidence for the term's continued public use, but it still does not
settle the glossary hypothesis. Merchandise can repeat an in-joke without defining
it, and a product card proves no encounter, sale, authorship, or current stock.

## How to use the working hypothesis

Researchers may test the possible coded reading when surrounding material concerns
dating or adult topics, while keeping the literal-food reading open. A mechanical
search-and-replace rule would create false claims.

| Evidence class | What it supports |
| --- | --- |
| Archive title or raw filename | The creator publicly used that label. |
| Machine-transcript context | A locator and interpretation to check, not a quotation. |
| Repeated contextual use | A working interpretation to test, with uncertainty stated. |
| Daniel's coded story | Daniel's account at most; never automatic proof about a partner. |

The concise glossary note is therefore: **an unresolved HIMR usage that researchers
sometimes interpret as a coded adult reference; direct audio and surrounding context
are required before applying that interpretation to any occurrence.**
